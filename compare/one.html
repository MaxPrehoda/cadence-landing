Cadence vs One (getone.one) two inboxes for your coding agents

One, the Mac app at getone.one, and Cadence look alike at first glance: a toolbar on the edge of the screen, an Inbox you answer with number keys, and voice on a double-tap of Option. Here is where they differ, and who should pick which.

Updated October 19, 2026 · Cadence Labs

Short answer

Cadence and One are close: both are Mac apps that put a dot for each coding agent on the edge of your screen, collect questions in an Inbox you answer with number keys, and route spoken requests on a double-tap of ⌥. The differences are in price, sign-in and where your requests go.

Pick Cadence if you want it free with no account and no daily limit, speech recognition on your Mac, and its AI features running on your own Claude Code login. Pick One if you have an Intel Mac, want a browser remote for a phone that is not an iPhone, or need agents on servers that stay reachable when your Mac is off, and you are fine with signing in and paying past 25 AI requests a day.

What they share

If you have used one, the other will feel familiar. Both apps:

  • show a dot per agent on the edge of the screen: blue working, amber needs you, green done;
  • collect questions and finished work in one Inbox, answered with number keys, with J/K to move and a two-second undo;
  • send a spoken or typed request to the right agent, or start a new one, when you double-tap ⌥;
  • attach part of your screen to a request;
  • run other CLIs in herdr, the free, open-source terminal runtime for coding agents;
  • have an iPhone app in TestFlight and a way to see agents on Linux servers.

Your coding agents run on your own subscriptions in both. The differences are in everything around them.

Where they differ

Price and account

Cadence is free, with no account and no subscription. One needs a sign-in with Apple or Google. Its free plan includes 25 requests a day to One's AI, which handles voice, routing, titles and recaps. Pro, at $12.99 a month, lists unlimited usage, and a limited Lifetime license costs $229, rising to $299.

Where your words and screenshots go

In One, each request sends your words, a screenshot and your agents' recent terminal output through One's server to AI providers. Its privacy policy names OpenAI, Anthropic and OpenRouter, and several speech-to-text services. One says it keeps no server-side archive of audio, screenshots or terminal text.

In Cadence, speech recognition runs on your Mac and audio is never uploaded. Talk routing, titles, recaps and the Director call Claude through the claude CLI you are already signed in to, so the request goes from your Mac to Anthropic under your own login. Cadence sends anonymous usage counts you can turn off; One's privacy policy says its app analytics go to PostHog with no opt-out in the app.

The flip side: Cadence's AI features need Claude Code installed and signed in. If you only use Codex and pay for ChatGPT, One can run its routing on that subscription instead.

Claude Code and the other CLIs

Cadence can host Claude Code itself. In Cadence chat mode, permission requests are real Allow/Deny cards, you see the cost, and each agent gets its own git worktree with Diff and History tabs. One runs Claude Code in herdr like any other CLI. Cadence also supports Cursor, Copilot, Gemini and Aider, which One's site does not list.

Dictation and the Director

Cadence's same key does a second job: hold ⌥ to dictate into any app, transcribed on your Mac. It also has the Director, a Claude session that can see every agent, answer "is anything stuck?", and start, message or stop agents when you ask.

Intel Macs, phones and servers

Here One does more. It runs on Intel Macs; Cadence is Apple Silicon only. Its phone app also runs on iPad, and any phone can use its browser remote. Its server connector keeps agents on up to eight Linux servers reachable when your Mac is off. Cadence reaches servers over SSH from your Mac, so its server agents reach your phone through your Mac.

Which one to pick

One is the better pick if

  • you use an Intel Mac;
  • you want to check agents from an Android phone, an iPad or a browser;
  • your agents run on servers and you want them reachable while your Mac sleeps;
  • you use Codex and ChatGPT, not Claude Code, and want the routing to run on that subscription.

Cadence is the better pick if

  • you want no account, no subscription and no daily request limit;
  • you want your voice transcribed on your Mac and your agents' output kept off third-party servers;
  • you use Claude Code and want real Allow/Deny cards, cost and worktree diffs;
  • you run Cursor, Copilot, Gemini or Aider next to Claude Code and Codex;
  • you also want dictation into every app.

Trying Cadence next to One

Both apps use a double-tap of ⌥, so quit One while you try Cadence, or change Cadence's key in Settings → Hotkey → Push-to-Talk Key (for example to ⌃ or ⌘⌥). Then:

  1. Download Cadence and open it. There is nothing to sign in to.
  2. Make sure the claude CLI is installed and signed in; Cadence uses that login.
  3. Open ⋯ → New agent… on the toolbar, pick a CLI and a folder, and start an agent. If herdr is missing, the view shows the install command.

Common questions

Is Cadence a free alternative to getone.one?

Yes. Cadence is free, with no account, no subscription and no daily request limit. It has the same core pieces as One: a status toolbar, a numbered Inbox and voice routing on a double-tap of Option. It runs on Apple Silicon Macs only.

Is One (getone.one) the same as withone.ai?

No. withone.ai is an unrelated product. This page compares Cadence with One, the Mac app for coding agents at getone.one.

Does One work on Intel Macs?

Yes. One's site lists macOS 13 or later on Apple Silicon or Intel. Cadence needs Apple Silicon and has no Intel build.

Do Cadence and One send my code to their servers?

One sends each request, including a screenshot and your agents' recent terminal output, through its server to AI providers. Cadence has no AI server of its own: its routing, titles, recaps and Director use Claude through your own Claude Code login. In both apps, your coding agents talk to their own providers under your own accounts.
